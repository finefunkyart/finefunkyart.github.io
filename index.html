Artist statement

I make work from what the body carries—memory, ancestry, womanhood, grief, survival, desire, and the stubborn force of becoming.

Across painting, mixed media, sculpture, and photography, I return to the figure as both vessel and witness. I am interested in the marks life leaves behind: wounds that become maps, fragments that become language, and color that refuses silence.

My work does not ask for a single interpretation. It asks the viewer to look once, then look again—to bring their own history into the encounter.

05 / Artist biography

Jaqulin Joseph

Artist. Photographer. Educator. Author. A woman making language from color, form, and lived experience.

Jaqulin Joseph is a New Orleans–based multidisciplinary artist whose practice encompasses painting, collage, drawing, mixed media, sculpture, assemblage, photography, and writing. Her work explores memory, identity, ancestry, the African American experience, feminine strength, intimacy, rupture, and survival.

Joseph began making art at nineteen and later studied Art and Creative Studies at the University of New Orleans. During her years in San Diego, she founded and operated Abstrax Arts Gallery, where she organized exhibitions, connected artists with collectors, taught art classes, and developed her work in professional and commercial photography.

Her experience as an educator and community liaison remains inseparable from her studio practice. She believes art can witness what history tries to erase, preserve personal and collective memory, create space for healing, and begin difficult conversations. Joseph is also the author and illustrator of Bizarre Coloring Book.

“Woman is the first form.”

Why I make

“I don’t paint what I see. I paint what I survived.”

I make art because silence is never empty. It contains memory, history, grief, beauty, anger, humor, and all the truths the body has learned to carry. My materials allow those truths to take form.

I return to women’s bodies because they are often treated as objects while holding entire worlds. In my work, the body is witness, archive, shelter, wound, and source. Texture becomes testimony. Fragments become language. Color refuses disappearance.

I am motivated by the people and histories that might otherwise be overlooked—especially the resilience of Black women, the inheritance of ancestry, and the human need to be fully seen. I create so that survival is not mistaken for silence and so that memory has somewhere to live.
