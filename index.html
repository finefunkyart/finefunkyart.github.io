Featured painting

Suddenly Sassy

Suddenly Sassy gathers several faces into one restless, expressive field. Eyes, lips, curves and fragments overlap and interrupt one another, suggesting the many selves a woman may carry at the same time: observant, playful, guarded, sensual, questioning and completely her own.

The bright yellows, reds, greens and turquoise refuse quietness. The figures do not ask permission to take up space. Instead, they meet the viewer directly, creating a conversation about personality, confidence, contradiction and the freedom to be more than one thing at once.

Rather than presenting a single portrait, the work becomes a chorus of moods. Its sass is not simply attitude; it is presence.

About the artist

Art as witness.
Art as memory.
Art as survival.

Jaqulin Joseph is a New Orleans–area visual artist, photographer, sculptor, author and educator whose practice moves across painting, mixed media, assemblage, bronze sculpture, photography and writing.

Her work is rooted in emotion, memory, observation, identity and transformation. She is especially interested in womanhood, ancestry, resilience, belonging, grief, history and the ways personal experience becomes physical material.

Joseph holds a Bachelor of Arts in Fine Arts from the University of New Orleans. Her professional life has included work as an art instructor, freelance photographer, gallery director and owner, assistant gallery director, art director, educator and community-engagement professional.

She founded and operated Abstrax Arts Gallery and Photography Studio in San Diego, where she developed exhibitions, connected artists with buyers, taught art and created visual communications. Whether making an image, building a sculpture, writing a book or teaching, she approaches creativity as an act of truth-telling, discovery and connection.
