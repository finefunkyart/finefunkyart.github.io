Dedication

This book is dedicated to the women who raised me, the child who grew me, and the Creator who never left me.

To my mother, Odessa —
You were my foundation.
You taught me how to stretch a dollar, speak with grace, cook with soul, and pray with purpose.
You never let me believe I was less than royal, even when life tried to convince me otherwise.
Your wisdom and balance still guide me.
This book carries your fingerprints.

To my son, Joshua —
My greatest love.
My reason, my rhythm, my why.
Though you are not here in body, your spirit dances through every word.
You taught me what unconditional love feels like.
You made me a mother.
You made me whole.

Introduction: A Love Letter to the Single Mother

This book was born from tears, triumph, and the kind of strength you can’t always see — but always feel.

I didn’t write this because I had it all figured out.
I wrote this because I lived it.

I was a young, single mother trying to do the impossible: raise a child, keep a roof overhead, feed a growing body and soul, and somehow keep my own spirit from breaking. There were days I had to be the chef, the nurse, the teacher, the counselor, the protector, and the provider — all before noon.

Nobody handed me a manual.
Nobody dropped off instructions on how to survive.
So I made one — for you.

6

This book is part blueprint, part comfort, and part fire.

It’s filled with recipes, real stories, life-saving resources, and reminders that you’re not alone.
You’ll find budgeting tips next to poems.
Grocery lists next to grace.
A little laughter. A little healing. A lot of truth.

Because the truth is:
You don’t have to be perfect.
You don’t have to be wealthy.
You just have to be willing —
to love your child, to believe in yourself, and to keep going.

7

Even on the days you cry.
Even on the days you mess up.
Even when nobody claps — you keep showing up.

That, mama, is love in its strongest form.

Welcome to A Single Mother's Survival Guide.
May it bless your hands, your heart, and your home.

With love,
Jaqulin Joseph

Chapter 1: The Reality of Raising Alone

Let’s start with the truth:

Being a single mother doesn’t make you broken.
It makes you a builder.

A builder of bedtime routines, mashed potato dinners, late-night prayers, and early-morning bus stops.
A builder of futures from limited paychecks.
A builder of peace when the world hands you storms.

Whether you are a single mother by choice, by heartbreak, by loss, or by divine assignment — you are not alone. You are part of a sacred sisterhood of women who carry entire households with just two hands and a heart full of hope.

You might wake up to silence — no partner to pour coffee or swap shifts with.
You might eat standing up while folding laundry, helping with homework, and checking your phone for tomorrow’s work schedule.
You might feel like the only adult in your child’s universe, and the pressure of that weight can feel unbearable.
