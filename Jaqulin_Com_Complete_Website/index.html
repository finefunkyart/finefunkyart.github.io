Selected Work

Paintings and assemblages rooted in memory, identity, history, the body, connection and transformation.

Mixed-media assemblage

I Have Seen. I Remember.

Look once. Then look again. The photographic film becomes more than film; it becomes memory — what we have seen, what has been done to us, what we have carried, and what refuses to disappear. The red may be blood, passion, suffering or life. There is no single answer waiting here. The work asks the viewer not only what they see, but what they take with them.

Mixed-media assemblage

Bloodlines: Still We Rise

The body of a woman becomes a vessel of memory. Burned cloth, red fabric and thread hold wounds, ancestry and survival. The work carries pain and healing together: we carry their wounds, their blood, their strength — and still, we rise.

Painting

Color Tribe

Color Tribe is about belonging without sameness. Figures, fragments and intersecting forms live together without surrendering individuality. Difference is not something that must be erased before connection can happen.

Painting

Somewhere Only We Know

A private landscape between memory and becoming. The work holds gratitude, love, loss, places, relationships and versions of ourselves that may now exist only inside us. Painting becomes a way to travel there.

Mixed-media relief painting

Katrina

Created in the aftermath of Hurricane Katrina, this work reflects loss, upheaval and survival. The empty garment becomes evidence of absence; the layered surface carries floodwater, debris, wounds and history. The water receded. The memory did not.

Painting

Read My Mind

Two blue faces occupy the space between expression and silence. Blue becomes an emotional language for closeness, longing, contemplation and distance. The work asks: how much can we truly know by looking at another person?

Painting

Abstract Bottles and Fruit

Familiar objects become rhythm, movement and architecture. Abstraction lets the ordinary turn slightly, allowing bottles, fruit, curves and negative space to become something newly seen.

Mixed-media abstract

What Remains Untranslated

Fragments of printed language live beneath paint, texture, earth tones, ochre and green. Parts emerge and disappear. The work considers inherited histories and stories we may never fully translate, yet still recognize as present.

Portrait / mixed media

Ibrahim Traoré — Burkina Faso

This work responds to questions of African strength, leadership, sovereignty and self-determination. It is not intended as propaganda; it holds the energy and questions of a contested political moment, asking who gets to write Africa’s future.

Bronze Figure Studies

Lost-wax cast bronze sculptures exploring womanhood, wholeness, connection, balance and inner strength.

Solo Female

Lost-wax cast bronze

She is not waiting to be completed. She stands whole in her own form — a figure of strength, vulnerability, creation and survival.

Committed to the Triangle

Lost-wax cast bronze

The triangle becomes a structure for body, mind and spirit — softness and strength held together. A woman can bend without breaking.

Embrace of the Women

Lost-wax cast bronze

An embrace becomes its own form: a place of refuge, recognition, tenderness and strength between women.

Rooted Essence

Lost-wax cast bronze

Grounded in the earth yet reaching inward, the figure speaks to inner strength, ancestral wisdom and the enduring feminine.

About the artist

Art as witness.
Art as memory.
Art as survival.

Jaqulin Joseph is a New Orleans–area visual artist, photographer, sculptor, author and educator whose practice moves across painting, mixed media, assemblage, bronze sculpture and photography.

Her work is rooted in emotion, memory, observation, identity and transformation. She is especially interested in womanhood, ancestry, resilience, belonging, grief, history and the ways personal experience becomes physical material.

Joseph holds a Bachelor of Arts in Fine Arts from the University of New Orleans. Her professional life has included work as an art instructor, freelance photographer, gallery director and owner, assistant gallery director, art director, educator and community-engagement professional. She founded and operated Abstrax Arts Gallery and Photography Studio in San Diego, developing exhibitions, connecting artists with buyers, teaching art and producing visual communications.

Whether making an image, building a sculpture, writing a book or teaching, she approaches creativity as an act of truth-telling, discovery and connection.
