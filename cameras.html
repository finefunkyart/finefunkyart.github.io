Private collection · photographic history

Vintage Cameras

A small museum of machines that once held light. These cameras trace more than a century of changing materials, mechanics, portability and the desire to preserve a moment.

The collection

Objects of photographic history

Each camera is presented individually so collectors can identify the model, read its story and inquire directly. Additional photographs, condition details and availability can be supplied upon request.

Collection 01

Zeiss Ikon Folding Camera

German folding camera · early 20th century

A compact bellows camera from the German precision-camera tradition. Its folding body, optical viewfinder and mechanical controls represent the period when serious photography was becoming more portable.

Collection 02

Rochester Premo B

Wooden folding/view camera · c. 1890s–early 1900s

A handsome American wooden camera from Rochester’s early photographic industry. The Premo family became known for finely built cameras that bridged studio practice and increasingly portable large-format photography.

Collection 03

Balda Baldinette

German folding camera · mid-20th century

A compact German folder built around a collapsible bellows design. Cameras like the Baldinette brought quality optics and precise mechanical shutters into a package small enough to travel easily.

Collection 04

Kodak Brownie 8 Movie Camera

8 mm home-movie camera · mid-20th century

Part of the era when moving pictures left the theater and entered family life. Brownie movie cameras made home filmmaking approachable, turning vacations, birthdays and ordinary days into personal archives.

Collection 05

Kodak Brownie Reflex

Twin-lens reflex-style camera · mid-20th century

With its large viewing lens above the taking lens, the Brownie Reflex brought the look and handling of a twin-lens camera to a broad audience. Its straightforward design made picture-making feel immediate and accessible.

Collection 06

Herco Imperial 620

Box camera · mid-20th century

A simple snapshot camera from the age of inexpensive roll-film photography. Its graphic faceplate and direct controls reflect the democratization of photography: point, frame, expose, remember.

Kodak Brownie Bullet

Box camera · mid-20th century

A compact Brownie-era snapshot camera with unmistakable streamlined styling. Cameras of this type were designed for uncomplicated everyday photography and helped make family picture-taking a routine part of American life.
