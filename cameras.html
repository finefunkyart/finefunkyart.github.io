Vintage Cameras

Tools that once held light now hold history. This selected collection traces changing ideas of portability, family photography, home movies, and mechanical craft. Individual pieces are available by inquiry.

Collection 01

Rochester Premo B

Large-format folding camera · c. 1890s–early 1900s

Built by the Rochester Optical Company in Rochester, New York, the Premo family belongs to the early age of portable large-format photography. The wooden body, leather bellows, brass fittings, and front movements reflect a period when the camera was both precision instrument and finely made object.

Maker: Rochester Optical Company · Format: 4 × 5 in · Materials: wood, leather bellows, metal hardware

Collection 02

Zeiss Ikon Folding Camera

German folding camera · early–mid 20th century

Zeiss Ikon became one of Germany’s major camera makers, known for compact folding cameras that placed precision lenses and shutters into portable bodies. This example preserves the mechanical elegance that made the folding camera a defining form of European photography.

Maker: Zeiss Ikon · Type: folding camera · Origin: Germany

Collection 03

Balda Baldinette

35mm folding camera · 1950s

The Baldinette represents the postwar German move toward compact 35mm photography. Balda combined a folding body with a Schneider-Kreuznach lens and Prontor shutter, creating a camera small enough for travel yet unmistakably mechanical in character.

Maker: Balda · Format: 35mm · Origin: Germany · Shutter family: Prontor

Collection 04

Kodak Brownie 8 Movie Camera

8mm home-movie camera · introduced 1951

Kodak introduced the low-priced Brownie 8mm Movie Camera in 1951, helping move motion-picture making further into everyday family life. The camera belongs to the era when birthdays, vacations, and ordinary domestic moments increasingly became moving-image records.

Maker: Eastman Kodak · Format: 8mm movie film · Introduced: 1951

Collection 05

Kodak Brownie Reflex Synchro

127 roll-film reflex-style camera · 1940s–1950s

The Brownie Reflex Synchro used a large brilliant finder and a twin-lens-reflex-inspired form while keeping the simple controls associated with the Brownie line. Its flash-synchronized version appeared during the 1940s and became part of Kodak’s long effort to make picture-taking simple and affordable.

Maker: Kodak · Film: 127 · Type: box roll-film / reflex-style finder

Herco Imperial 620 Snap Shot Camera

Box camera · mid-20th century

Simple box cameras brought photography into homes by reducing picture-making to a few straightforward actions. The Herco Imperial’s bold faceplate and 620-film format place it firmly within that democratic snapshot tradition.

Brand: Herco Imperial · Film: 620 · Type: snapshot box camera

Collection 07

Kodak Brownie Bullet

127 roll-film camera · 1957–1964

The Brownie Bullet was a promotional version of Kodak’s Brownie Holiday design. Its molded body, eye-level finder, and simple operation capture the optimistic, accessible character of postwar amateur photography.

Maker: Kodak · Film: 127 · Produced: 1957–1964 · Type: solid-body roll-film camera
