Rochester Premo B

Folding plate camera · Rochester, New York · c. 1894–1902

The Premo B came from Rochester Optical Company during Rochester’s rise as a major American camera-making center. Built in mahogany with polished metal fittings, bellows, a folding bed and camera movements, it represents the transition from large studio equipment toward more portable serious photography.

late 1890s–early 1900s
Collection 02

POCO Folding Large-Format Plate Camera

Folding plate camera · Rochester Camera Co. · Rochester, New York · late 1890s–early 1900s

Made during Rochester’s emergence as a center of American camera manufacturing, this POCO is a mahogany folding plate camera with dark bellows, brass hardware and period metal fittings. Its surviving front standard bears the POCO Rochester Camera Co. name, while the shutter is marked Bausch & Lomb Optical Co., Rochester, New York.

The Rochester camera concern traces to H. B. Carlton’s Rochester Camera Manufacturing Company, established in 1891. In 1899 it became part of the merger that formed Rochester Optical & Camera Company. George Eastman acquired the combined concern in 1903, bringing this camera-making lineage into the company that became Eastman Kodak. More than a century later, the worn leather, polished wood and engraved metalwork still carry the marks of photography’s early portable era.

c. late 1920s–early 1930s
Collection 04

Zeiss Ikon Folding Camera

Folding camera · Zeiss Ikon · Germany · c. late 1920s–early 1930s

Zeiss Ikon was established in 1926, bringing several important German camera-making traditions together under one name. This example carries a Contessa-Nettel-marked Nettel-Anastigmat lens and a Klio shutter, preserving the lineage of one of the companies that became part of the Zeiss Ikon story.

Its collapsible bellows, folding bed, leatherette body and engraved metal fittings belong to the interwar era when capable cameras were becoming compact enough to travel. The surviving wear and original markings make this piece both a photographic instrument and a record of the transition from earlier German camera makers into the Zeiss Ikon era.

Collection 19

Canon AE-1 Program

35mm SLR · Canon · Japan · 1981–1985

Introduced in 1981, the Canon AE-1 Program refined Canon’s enormously successful AE-1 concept with a fully automatic Program exposure mode while retaining shutter-priority and manual control. Built around Canon’s FD lens mount, it helped make sophisticated 35mm SLR photography approachable to a broad generation of photographers.

This collection example is shown with a long telephoto zoom lens, giving the compact silver-and-black body a striking working-photographer profile. The AE-1 Program remains one of the most recognizable cameras of the early electronic SLR era, bridging classic mechanical controls and increasingly automated exposure.
